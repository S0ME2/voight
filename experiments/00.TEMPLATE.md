# Experiment 00 — Report template and editorial standard

This file is the standard every experiment report in this directory follows.
It is itself edited against the four questions below; a section, table, or
figure that cannot answer them does not belong in a report.

## Editorial law (apply to every section, table, and figure)

1. **Why?** — states what decision the content serves. A report exists to
   record a measured decision and what it cost, not to describe activity.
2. **At what cost?** — states the trade-off of the finding or of including the
   content. Adoption costs, maintenance costs, and reader time are all costs.
3. **Does it fit?** — matches the report's scope and the corpus actually
   measured. No general claims from a 20-document corpus.
4. **What am I removing?** — every revision states what was cut and why, in the
   Changelog. Nothing disappears silently.

Hard rules:

- Every number traces to a file under `outputs/` (cited in §Raw data
  disposition) or is explicitly marked as reconstructed.
- **Figures are keyed, not decorative — Pareto, not minimalism.** Every chart
  that carries a decision-relevant shape a table cannot (a frontier, a knee, a
  trade-off, a divergence) belongs in the report; charts that merely restate
  their own table do not.
- **The image carries data only**: marks, legend, axis names, and direct value
  labels. Titles, experiment names, descriptions, and caveats are written
  *outside* the image as the report's figure caption — never inside the PNG.
- Negative results are recorded in §Negative results, not discarded: a rejected
  candidate with its rejection reason is information; an unrecorded one is a
  future re-run.
- Status vocabulary, one per report: `current` (basis of a live decision),
  `superseded` (numerically or methodologically replaced — name the successor),
  `historical` (context only, no surviving default).

## 1. Question and why it mattered

The decision at stake, stated before the answer: what would have been done
differently if the answer came out the other way.

## 2. Method

What was varied, what was held fixed, how many repeats, what was measured and
where the timing boundary is. Enough to reproduce and to audit; not a tutorial.

## 3. Results

The key tables, then the keyed figures. Every figure caption states what to
look at and which decision it supports.

## 4. Interpretation

What the numbers mean. Effect sizes next to baselines, and the cost side of
every recommended change (latency, memory, accuracy, complexity).

## 5. Decision and consequence

What changed as a result — config, code, defaults — with decision IDs. Also
what deliberately did **not** change, and why.

## 6. Negative results and dead ends

Tried and rejected, with reasons. Includes candidates that helped one document
type but regressed another, and measurement approaches that proved unreliable.

## 7. Limits

What this does not establish: corpus size and composition, provenance gaps,
machine specificity. Repeated once per report, not per claim.

## 8. Raw data disposition

The accounting that makes `outputs/` deletable. One row per directory this
experiment produced:

| Path | Size | Status | Safe to delete after this report |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `outputs/benchmarks/<family>/<run>` | NN MB | primary evidence / duplicate rerun / superseded / broken | yes — every decision-grade number is embedded above / no — … |

`duplicate rerun` means an identical re-execution of a cited run (identical
manifest, byte-identical payloads); `superseded` means an earlier run of the
same family replaced by a later corrected one.
